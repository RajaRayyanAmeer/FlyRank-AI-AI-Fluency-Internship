# FlyRank AI Fluency Internship — Week 1, Task 3
## Personal Workflow Audit

**Primary direction:** AI systems for robotics / the physical world

| # | Recurring task from my real week | Classification | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Debugging hardware/software integration when a project does not work | **Collaborate with AI** | I usually search Google, YouTube and AI, then experiment and verify the suggestions myself. AI is useful for generating possible causes, but I need to test them on the real hardware. |
| 2 | Learning an unfamiliar technology needed for a project | **Collaborate with AI** | I can ask AI to explain unfamiliar concepts, compare approaches, create examples and identify what I need to learn, while I verify the information and apply it myself. |
| 3 | Writing or improving technical code for a project | **Delegate to AI with review** | AI can generate boilerplate, suggest implementations or identify possible bugs, but I need to understand, test and modify the code before using it. |
| 4 | Designing how different hardware/software components should work together | **Collaborate with AI** | AI can challenge my architecture and suggest alternatives, but I need to make the final engineering decision because I understand the project constraints and physical setup. |
| 5 | Searching for documentation, examples and possible solutions to a technical problem | **Delegate to AI with review** | AI can quickly organize possible solutions and search directions, but technical documentation and real-world tests must verify the result. |
| 6 | Testing whether a physical prototype actually works | **Just me** | AI cannot physically observe the complete setup or guarantee that the hardware behaves correctly. I need to run the experiment and judge the real result. |
| 7 | Making the final decision about whether an AI-generated technical solution should be used | **Just me** | AI is a companion rather than the authority. I remain responsible for deciding whether a solution is technically appropriate and safe. |
| 8 | Working on ML data cleaning, feature engineering and model experiments | **Collaborate with AI** | AI can suggest transformations, explain methods and help interpret results, but I need to understand the data, run the experiments and evaluate whether the results are actually meaningful. |
| 9 | Comparing ML models and interpreting evaluation results | **Collaborate with AI** | AI can help explain metrics and challenge my interpretation, but I must verify the calculations and decide whether the model is actually suitable for the problem. |
| 10 | Building or improving my personal Python/AI-agent project | **Collaborate with AI** | I can use AI for brainstorming, implementation ideas, debugging and research while retaining control over the architecture and final behavior of the project. |
| 11 | Writing prompts for AI when I am stuck on a technical problem | **Collaborate with AI** | Prompting is part of my problem-solving process. I provide the context, constraints and what I have already tried, then use AI's response to continue the investigation. |
| 12 | Writing my personal story/novel | **Just me** | The creative direction, ideas, voice and personal meaning should remain mine. AI may be useful for limited feedback later, but I do not want it to become the author of my work. |
| 13 | Preparing explanations or documentation for technical projects | **Delegate to AI with review** | AI can turn rough technical notes into a clearer structure, but I must verify the technical accuracy and make sure the explanation represents what I actually built. |
| 14 | Researching what is happening in AI, robotics, computer vision and related fields | **Collaborate with AI** | AI can help me explore unfamiliar developments and organize information, but I should verify important claims through reliable sources before treating them as knowledge. |
| 15 | Making final decisions about my career direction and what I personally want to build | **Just me** | AI can challenge my thinking and expose alternatives, but this decision depends on my own goals, values and interests and should not be delegated. |

### What I notice from this audit

Most of my technical work falls into **collaboration rather than full delegation**. I use AI as a companion for research, debugging, brainstorming and implementation, but I still want to understand what is happening and test the result myself. This matches how I already described my AI use: I do not want AI to act as my boss or make decisions for me.

The tasks I want to keep primarily human are the ones involving **physical verification, final engineering judgment, personal creative work, and personal decisions**. These are areas where responsibility or personal ownership matters more than speed.
